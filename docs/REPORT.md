## Abstract

FaceGuard is a local desktop tool that detects faces in photos and short
videos, lets the user choose which faces to hide and correct misses, and
exports a redacted copy without any network access. It combines a pretrained
convolutional face detector (YuNet), sparse pyramidal Lucas-Kanade optical
flow on Shi-Tomasi corners, IoU-based track association, backward tracking
of newly found faces, and elliptical masks for blur, pixelation or solid fill.
We evaluate it by *leak frames*: the number of frames in which any face is
less than 90% covered, because a single exposed frame defeats a redaction
tool. On a 20 s synthetic clip with exact per-frame ground truth, detecting
every 10 frames and tracking in between ran at 1.50x the throughput of
per-frame detection (112.7 vs 75.2 FPS) and reduced leak frames from 43 to 0,
because tracking covers frames in which the detector misses rotated faces.
On a second clip built to stress occlusion and entry, the same setting first
leaked 8 frames while a new face entered between detections. Tracking each
new face backwards over the last 10 buffered frames removed all of them.
Finally, we attacked our own output with a face recogniser: at the original
blur strength, 52 of 76 redacted faces were re-identified among 76
candidates. This led to stronger defaults, at which none were.

## 1. Problem

People publishing event, classroom or street footage capture bystanders who
did not consent. Manual frame-by-frame redaction is slow enough that it is
skipped, and cloud tools require uploading the very faces that should be
protected. Automated detection helps, but detectors miss faces, so the tool
must make misses visible and correctable, and its evaluation must count
exposed frames rather than average accuracy.

Objectives (from the proposal): an image workflow with detection, selection,
correction and export; a video workflow that compares per-frame detection
with periodic detection plus tracking; and an evaluation of detection errors,
redaction coverage, speed and failure cases.

## 3. System

FaceGuard is a single local Python application: `core/` holds all vision
logic and imports no UI code, `cli.py` runs it headless, `ui/app.py` is a
Streamlit front end bound to localhost with usage statistics disabled, and
`eval/` holds annotation, metrics and experiments.

Video processing is split into **analyse** (decode, detect, track, associate;
stores only boxes per track per frame) and **render** (decode again and
redact the stored boxes of the selected tracks). Render never re-runs
detection, so the person the user unticks is exactly the person left visible
(D-010). Frames are streamed; a 30 s 720p clip is never held in memory (peak
RSS of the whole experiment run: see `docs/RESULTS.md`).

**Review and correction (UI).** Numbered overlays with scores; per-track
redact toggle and delete; a frame scrubber with side-by-side original and
redacted previews; manual boxes entered as coordinates with a live preview,
then followed by the tracker, held still to an end frame, or interpolated to
a second keyframe box (D-015). Export re-encodes the image (no EXIF copied)
or the video to H.264; audio is dropped unless the user opts in, with a
warning that voices can identify people. Reset deletes the session's
temporary folder; stale folders from crashed sessions are removed at launch.

## 4. Methods

**Detection.** YuNet (`face_detection_yunet_2026may.onnx`, the 2023mar
weights with dynamic input size for OpenCV 5, SHA-256 pinned) at score
threshold 0.6 and NMS 0.3. `setInputSize` is updated for every new frame
size. A Haar cascade (Viola-Jones) is the classical baseline behind the same
interface.

**Tracking.** Up to 40 Shi-Tomasi corners are seeded inside each face box.
Pyramidal LK (4 levels, 21 px window) tracks them; a forward-backward check
discards points that do not return within 1 px. The box moves by the median
displacement and scales by the median ratio of pairwise distances. Flow runs
on a crop of the box plus a margin of one box size + 128 px, not on the full
frame: the first implementation built two full-frame pyramids per face per
call and was slower than per-frame detection (D-014).

**Association and lifecycle.** All trackers update first, then detections
are matched greedily by IoU ≥ 0.3 and re-anchor matched tracks. A followed
track survives 60 frames without a detector match; a lost one keeps its
frozen box for 15 frames; both stay redacted (D-012).

**When to detect.** Every N = 10 frames, and additionally (D-017): after a
tracker loses a face, and every 3 frames while a tracked face went unmatched
by the last detection. Scene cuts, detected by a simultaneous drop in grey
histogram correlation and in normalised cross-correlation of a 32 px
thumbnail, end all tracks and trigger detection (D-018).

**Redaction.** Each box is padded by 0.25 per side; an ellipse inscribed in
the padded box is drawn, blurred, and mapped by `clip(2·blur, 0, 1)` so the
inside is fully opaque and the soft edge lies outside (D-007). Padding
≥ (√2 − 1)/2 ≈ 0.207 is the geometric condition for the ellipse to contain
the face box's corners (D-006). Blur kernels and pixel blocks scale with
face size.

## 5. Evaluation design

- **Leak frames** (headline): frames with at least one ground-truth face
  under 90% mask coverage, reported as a raw count, overall and per segment.
- **Coverage**: fraction of each ground-truth box under the union of applied
  masks (alpha ≥ 0.5), computed with the renderer's own mask function.
- **Precision, recall, F1** at IoU 0.5 with one-to-one matching; a PR curve
  and average precision from a single low-threshold pass.
- **Throughput**: frames per second of the processing loop, 3 runs, mean ±
  sample standard deviation; detector calls; peak memory.
- **Identity**: track IDs per person, ID consistency, ID switches.

Experiments E1–E7 are defined in `docs/EVALUATION.md` and
`eval/experiments.yaml`; one command regenerates every table and plot.

## 6. Data

| Set | Content | Ground truth |
|---|---|---|
| `synthetic_er` (600 frames) | A public 6-person photo animated through static, slow pan, ±40° roll, whip pan, low light with noise, zoom-out to ~17 px faces | Exact, every frame |
| `synthetic_cross` (450 frames) | Three face patches on a plain background: two walk past each other (one fully hidden), one enters from the edge | Exact, every frame; a face counts while ≥ 50% visible |
| Stills (40 images, 240 faces) | Every 15th frame of `synthetic_er`, tagged by segment | Exact |

**No hand-annotated real media is included yet.** The faces are real
photographs, but the motion is 2D, there is no motion blur or out-of-plane
head rotation, and there are only a handful of distinct people. The
synthetic sets also contain no non-face clutter, so they cannot measure false
positives meaningfully. These results show *mechanisms*, not field accuracy.

## 7. Results

### 7.1 Tracking makes redaction both faster and safer (E2, E3, E12)

| Clip | Configuration | FPS | Detector calls | Leak frames | Track IDs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| synthetic_er | per-frame detection | 75.2 ± 0.7 | 600 | **43** | 174 |
| synthetic_er | every 10 + LK | 112.7 ± 0.7 | 72 | **0** | 6 |
| synthetic_cross | per-frame detection | 78.9 ± 1.4 | 450 | **0** | 4 |
| synthetic_cross | every 10 + LK, no backfill | 173.3 ± 2.8 | 61 | **8** | 4 |
| synthetic_cross | every 10 + LK + backfill 10 (default) | 158.2 ± 0.3 | 61 | **0** | 4 |

All 43 per-frame leaks on `synthetic_er` are in the rotation segment, where
YuNet misses faces rolled about 40°. Per-frame detection has no memory, so
each miss is an exposed frame and a new ID (174 IDs for 6 people). LK keeps
following those faces. Leak frames therefore went *down* while the detector
ran 8x less often.

On `synthetic_cross`, without backfill, the 8 leaks were all in the entry
segment: a new face is visible before the next scheduled detection, and no
forward tracker can cover a face nobody has detected yet. Backfill (D-026)
looks *backwards* instead. The last 10 frames are kept in memory, and when a
face is first detected its tracker runs from that detection back through
them. This works because optical flow's brightness-constancy assumption
holds in either direction of time. E12: 5 frames of backfill leave 3
entry leaks, 10 or more leave none. The cost is 18 backfilled face-frames
that start slightly before the ground truth counts the face as visible
(precision 0.944 → 0.935), memory for 10 frames, and a writer that runs 10
frames behind the analysis.

With backfill on, every tracker except "hold still" (`none`, 56 and 39 leak
frames) has 0 leaks on both clips. CSRT runs at 20.4 FPS, KCF at 50.7 FPS
with 116 IDs for 6 people on `synthetic_er`, and LK at 112.7 FPS.

### 7.2 Early detection fixes occlusion; backfill now does too (E7)

Before backfill, on `synthetic_cross` at N = 10 (measured 2026-11-14, before D-026):

| Policy | Detector calls | Leak frames (crossing / entry) |
|---|---|---|
| scheduled only | 45 | 13 (5 / 8) |
| + after a lost track | 45 | 13 (5 / 8) |
| + every 3 frames while unconfirmed | 61 | 8 (0 / 8) |
| + every frame while unconfirmed | 91 | 8 (0 / 8) |

Tracing the crossing leak showed that the hidden face's track was never
"lost": its points latched onto the shoulder of the person in front and
followed them. The rule that helps is disagreement: a face the tracker
reports but the detector did not find. Checking every 3 frames removed the
crossing leaks at 6% of throughput.

With backfill on, all four policies have **0 leak frames** (E7 in
RESULTS.md). A face that re-emerges from behind an occluder is a new track,
so it is backfilled over the frames where it was visible but not yet
re-detected. The early-detection rule is kept because it re-acquires faces
sooner and keeps IDs stable, but on these clips it is no longer what
prevents the leak.

### 7.3 Detectors (E1, E4, E5)

| Detector | Precision | Recall | F1 | ms / image |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YuNet | 1.000 | 0.975 | 0.987 | 10.1 |
| Haar | 0.745 | 0.829 | 0.785 | 25.5 |

YuNet was both more accurate and 2.5x faster than the Haar cascade on this
CPU. Haar's recall collapses on rolled faces (0.50) and faces under 40 px
(0.44); YuNet's only misses are rolled faces (recall 0.875 in that segment).
YuNet's average precision on the stills is 0.992. Lowering the threshold to
0.3 raised recall from 0.975 to 0.983 with no false positives, but these
stills contain no face-like clutter, so the shipped default stays at 0.6
until real annotated images can show the false-positive cost.

### 7.4 Padding (E6)

| Padding | `synthetic_er` coverage | Leak frames | Mask area / box area |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.834 | 600 | 0.89 |
| 0.10 | 0.966 | 32 | 1.26 |
| 0.20 | 0.999 | 0 | 1.70 |
| 0.25 (default) | 1.000 | 0 | 1.93 |
| 0.30 | 1.000 | 0 | 2.19 |

The measured step between 0.1 and 0.2 matches the geometric threshold of
0.207. The default 0.25 leaves a margin for imperfect boxes at the cost of
hiding about twice the detector box area.

### 7.5 Attacking the output: are redacted faces still identifiable? (E13)

Coverage says a mask was applied, not that it works. For each of the 76
faces in the real photos, we redacted the image and then attacked the
output. YuNet was re-run at 0.3, and SFace compared the redacted face with
its original, using the original landmarks for alignment (the attacker's
best case).

| Setting | Still detectable (0.3) | Matchable (cos > 0.363) | Re-identified, rank 1 of 76 |
|---|---|---|---|
| blur 0.35 (old default) | 75 | 43 | **52** |
| blur 1.0 (new default) | 69 | 0 | 0 |
| pixelate 8 blocks (old default) | 33 | 1 | 3 |
| pixelate 5 blocks (new default) | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| fill | 2 | 0 | 1 |

At the old default strength, blur was not anonymisation in any sense. The
defaults were changed on this evidence (D-027). Detection and
identification differ: full-strength blur still *looks like a face* to
YuNet (69 of 76), but SFace matched none of them. Fill's single rank-1 hit
is chance level. The same check now runs on every export (D-028).

### 7.6 What did not help (E14, E15)

- **Hungarian association** gave results identical to greedy on both clips
  with both trackers, which confirms D-009 with data (D-029).
- **Kalman coasting** of lost tracks: LK never lost a face, so every motion
  model was identical. With KCF, moving a lost box made leaks worse (freeze 0,
  velocity 11, Kalman 12), because the frozen box was already where the face
  reappeared (D-030).
- **Profile-face cascade**: our data contains no profile faces, so it found
  nothing new and added 6 false positives on the synthetic stills, at about
  6x the detection time (D-032). It needs a profile test set.
- **Re-ID linking** did help: it removed the one ID switch on the crossing
  clip (similarity 0.96) for both LK and KCF. With KCF on `synthetic_er` it
  made 74 links, 1 of which joined two different people, so it is opt-in
  (D-031).

### 7.7 Robustness to degraded video (E16)

| `synthetic_er` condition | Leak frames, every 10 + LK | Leak frames, per-frame detection |
|---|---|---|
| original | 0 | 43 |
| low light (30% + noise) | 0 | 114 |
| JPEG quality 12 | 0 | 48 |
| 11 px motion blur | 0 | 63 |
| half resolution | 14 | 80 |

Tracking absorbs most degradations that make the detector miss
intermittently. Half resolution is the exception: the smallest faces fall
to about 12 px, below YuNet's reliable range, so there is nothing to track.

### 7.8 Speed for long videos (D-036)

Splitting a 3,000-frame clip into chunks analysed in parallel processes,
with a 30-frame warm-up per chunk, gave 279 FPS with 4 workers against
160 FPS sequential, with identical metrics. A first version seeked to each
chunk's start; on one file the seek landed on the wrong frame and leaked 43
frames. Workers now reach their start by decoding forward.

## 8. Limitations and failure cases

- **Synthetic-only video evaluation.** See section 6. Real footage adds
  motion blur, out-of-plane rotation (profile faces, which YuNet frequently
  misses), compression and clutter. E16 simulates some of this; it does not
  replace real annotated clips.
- **Small faces at low resolution.** At half resolution, 14 frames leak on
  `synthetic_er` (E16).
- **Track IDs are not identities.** A person who is fully hidden and
  reappears gets a new ID unless re-ID linking is on (D-031), so selective
  redaction must be re-checked after occlusions and at scene cuts.
- **Frozen boxes.** When a tracker loses a moving face, its frozen box stays
  for 15 frames and may no longer cover the face. Coasting did not fix this
  on our data (section 7.6).
- **Backfill depth.** A face that stays undetected for longer than the
  buffer (10 frames) before its first detection is exposed for the frames
  before that.
- **Redaction strength.** Pixelation at 5 blocks and full-strength blur
  defeated our recogniser, but stronger attackers exist. Only fill destroys
  the information, and face redaction alone does not hide clothing or
  context. Voice pitch shifting is reversible (D-033).
- **Unmeasured extras.** Text redaction and the profile cascade have no
  annotated test sets yet.
- **Hardware.** Timings are from an Apple M5, faster than the target
  mid-range laptop; ratios are the transferable result.

## 9. Conclusion

Periodic detection with optical-flow tracking was faster than per-frame
detection on both clips and, where the detector missed hard poses, it was
also safer: it removed every leaked frame on the first clip and stayed
leak-free under low light, heavy compression and motion blur. Its remaining
cost, exposure of faces that appear between detections, was removed by
tracking each new face backwards through a short frame buffer. Attacking the
output with a face recogniser showed that the original blur default was
re-identifiable for most faces, and the defaults were changed accordingly.
Several plausible extensions (Hungarian matching, Kalman coasting, a profile
cascade) made no difference or made things worse on our data, and are
reported as such. The leak-frame metric and the output attack are what made
these trade-offs visible.
