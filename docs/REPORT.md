## Abstract

FaceGuard is a local desktop tool that detects faces in photos and short
videos, lets the user choose which faces to hide and correct misses, and
exports a redacted copy without any network access. It combines a pretrained
convolutional face detector (YuNet), sparse pyramidal Lucas-Kanade optical
flow on Shi-Tomasi corners, IoU-based track association, and elliptical
masks for blur, pixelation or solid fill. We evaluate it by *leak frames*,
the number of frames in which any face is less than 90% covered, because a
single exposed frame defeats a redaction tool. On a 20 s synthetic clip with
exact per-frame ground truth, detecting every 10 frames and tracking in
between ran at 1.54x the throughput of per-frame detection (119.6 vs
77.5 FPS) and reduced leak frames from 43 to 0, because tracking covers
frames in which the detector misses rotated faces. On a second clip built to
stress occlusion and entry, the same setting ran 2.08x faster but leaked
8 frames, all while a new face entered between scheduled detections.

## 1. Problem

People publishing event, classroom or street footage capture bystanders who
did not consent. Manual frame-by-frame redaction is slow enough that it is
skipped, and cloud tools require uploading the very faces that should be
protected. Automated detection helps, but detectors miss faces, so the tool
must make misses visible and correctable, and its evaluation must count
exposed frames rather than average accuracy.

Objectives (from the proposal): an image workflow with detection, selection,
correction and export; a video workflow that compares per-frame detection
with periodic detection plus tracking; and an evaluation of detection errors,
redaction coverage, speed and failure cases.

## 3. System

FaceGuard is a single local Python application: `core/` holds all vision
logic and imports no UI code, `cli.py` runs it headless, `ui/app.py` is a
Streamlit front end bound to localhost with usage statistics disabled, and
`eval/` holds annotation, metrics and experiments.

Video processing is split into **analyse** (decode, detect, track, associate;
stores only boxes per track per frame) and **render** (decode again and
redact the stored boxes of the selected tracks). Render never re-runs
detection, so the person the user unticks is exactly the person left visible
(D-010). Frames are streamed; a 30 s 720p clip is never held in memory (peak
RSS of the whole experiment run: see `docs/RESULTS.md`).

**Review and correction (UI).** Numbered overlays with scores; per-track
redact toggle and delete; a frame scrubber with side-by-side original and
redacted previews; manual boxes entered as coordinates with a live preview,
then followed by the tracker, held still to an end frame, or interpolated to
a second keyframe box (D-015). Export re-encodes the image (no EXIF copied)
or the video to H.264; audio is dropped unless the user opts in, with a
warning that voices can identify people. Reset deletes the session's
temporary folder; stale folders from crashed sessions are removed at launch.

## 4. Methods

**Detection.** YuNet (`face_detection_yunet_2026may.onnx`, the 2023mar
weights with dynamic input size for OpenCV 5, SHA-256 pinned) at score
threshold 0.6 and NMS 0.3. `setInputSize` is updated for every new frame
size. A Haar cascade (Viola-Jones) is the classical baseline behind the same
interface.

**Tracking.** Up to 40 Shi-Tomasi corners are seeded inside each face box.
Pyramidal LK (4 levels, 21 px window) tracks them; a forward-backward check
discards points that do not return within 1 px. The box moves by the median
displacement and scales by the median ratio of pairwise distances. Flow runs
on a crop of the box plus a margin of one box size + 128 px, not on the full
frame: the first implementation built two full-frame pyramids per face per
call and was slower than per-frame detection (D-014).

**Association and lifecycle.** All trackers update first, then detections
are matched greedily by IoU ≥ 0.3 and re-anchor matched tracks. A followed
track survives 60 frames without a detector match; a lost one keeps its
frozen box for 15 frames; both stay redacted (D-012).

**When to detect.** Every N = 10 frames, and additionally (D-017): after a
tracker loses a face, and every 3 frames while a tracked face went unmatched
by the last detection. Scene cuts, detected by a simultaneous drop in grey
histogram correlation and in normalised cross-correlation of a 32 px
thumbnail, end all tracks and trigger detection (D-018).

**Redaction.** Each box is padded by 0.25 per side; an ellipse inscribed in
the padded box is drawn, blurred, and mapped by `clip(2·blur, 0, 1)` so the
inside is fully opaque and the soft edge lies outside (D-007). Padding
≥ (√2 − 1)/2 ≈ 0.207 is the geometric condition for the ellipse to contain
the face box's corners (D-006). Blur kernels and pixel blocks scale with
face size.

## 5. Evaluation design

- **Leak frames** (headline): frames with at least one ground-truth face
  under 90% mask coverage, reported as a raw count, overall and per segment.
- **Coverage**: fraction of each ground-truth box under the union of applied
  masks (alpha ≥ 0.5), computed with the renderer's own mask function.
- **Precision, recall, F1** at IoU 0.5 with one-to-one matching; a PR curve
  and average precision from a single low-threshold pass.
- **Throughput**: frames per second of the processing loop, 3 runs, mean ±
  sample standard deviation; detector calls; peak memory.
- **Identity**: track IDs per person, ID consistency, ID switches.

Experiments E1–E7 are defined in `docs/EVALUATION.md` and
`eval/experiments.yaml`; one command regenerates every table and plot.

## 6. Data

| Set | Content | Ground truth |
|---|---|---|
| `synthetic_er` (600 frames) | A public 6-person photo animated through static, slow pan, ±40° roll, whip pan, low light with noise, zoom-out to ~17 px faces | Exact, every frame |
| `synthetic_cross` (450 frames) | Three face patches on a plain background: two walk past each other (one fully hidden), one enters from the edge | Exact, every frame; a face counts while ≥ 50% visible |
| Stills (40 images, 240 faces) | Every 15th frame of `synthetic_er`, tagged by segment | Exact |

**No hand-annotated real media is included yet.** The faces are real
photographs, but the motion is 2D, there is no motion blur or out-of-plane
head rotation, and there are only a handful of distinct people. The
synthetic sets also contain no non-face clutter, so they cannot measure false
positives meaningfully. These results show *mechanisms*, not field accuracy.

## 7. Results

### 7.1 Tracking makes redaction both faster and safer (E2, E3)

| Clip | Configuration | FPS | Detector calls | Leak frames | Track IDs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| synthetic_er | per-frame detection | 77.5 ± 1.0 | 600 | **43** | 174 |
| synthetic_er | every 10 + LK | 119.6 ± 1.4 | 72 | **0** | 6 |
| synthetic_cross | per-frame detection | 82.6 ± 0.7 | 450 | **0** | 4 |
| synthetic_cross | every 10 + LK | 171.8 ± 1.3 | 61 | **8** | 4 |

All 43 per-frame leaks on `synthetic_er` are in the rotation segment, where
YuNet misses faces rolled about 40°. Per-frame detection has no memory, so
each miss is an exposed frame and a new ID (174 IDs for 6 people). LK keeps
following those faces. Leak frames therefore went *down* while the detector
ran 8x less often.

On `synthetic_cross` the trade-off reverses: the 8 leaks are all in the
entry segment, where a new face is visible before the next scheduled
detection. No tracking policy can cover a face nobody has detected yet;
only the interval matters (3 leaks at N = 5, 8 at N ≥ 10).

Holding boxes still between detections (`none`) is fastest and worst
(106 and 62 leak frames). CSRT nearly matches LK's coverage (3 leaks) at about a
sixth of its speed (20.9 FPS); KCF loses rolled and fast faces (10 leaks, 116 IDs on
`synthetic_er`).

### 7.2 Early detection fixes occlusion, not entry (E7)

| Policy (`synthetic_cross`, N = 10) | FPS | Detector calls | Leak frames (crossing / entry) |
|---|---|---|---|
| scheduled only | 182.8 | 45 | 13 (5 / 8) |
| + after a lost track | 181.9 | 45 | 13 (5 / 8) |
| + every 3 frames while unconfirmed | 171.8 | 61 | 8 (0 / 8) |
| + every frame while unconfirmed | 153.6 | 91 | 8 (0 / 8) |

Tracing the crossing leak showed that the hidden face's track was never
"lost": its points latched onto the shoulder of the person in front and
followed them. The lost-track rule therefore never fired on either clip. The
rule that helps is disagreement: a face the tracker reports but the detector
did not find. Checking every 3 frames removes the crossing leaks at 6% of
throughput; checking every frame costs 16% for no further gain. A first
attempt, detecting while face boxes overlap, never fired because the
occluder hides the face before the boxes meet, and was dropped.

### 7.3 Detectors (E1, E4, E5)

| Detector | Precision | Recall | F1 | ms / image |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YuNet | 1.000 | 0.975 | 0.987 | 9.7 |
| Haar | 0.745 | 0.829 | 0.785 | 24.4 |

YuNet was both more accurate and 2.5x faster than the Haar cascade on this
CPU. Haar's recall collapses on rolled faces (0.50) and faces under 40 px
(0.44); YuNet's only misses are rolled faces (recall 0.875 in that segment).
YuNet's average precision on the stills is 0.992. Lowering the threshold to
0.3 raised recall from 0.975 to 0.983 with no false positives, but these
stills contain no face-like clutter, so the shipped default stays at 0.6
until real annotated images can show the false-positive cost.

### 7.4 Padding (E6)

| Padding | `synthetic_er` coverage | Leak frames | Mask area / box area |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.834 | 600 | 0.89 |
| 0.10 | 0.966 | 32 | 1.26 |
| 0.20 | 0.999 | 0 | 1.70 |
| 0.25 (default) | 1.000 | 0 | 1.93 |
| 0.30 | 1.000 | 0 | 2.19 |

The measured step between 0.1 and 0.2 matches the geometric threshold of
0.207. The default 0.25 leaves a margin for imperfect boxes at the cost of
hiding about twice the detector box area.

## 8. Limitations and failure cases

- **Synthetic-only evaluation.** See section 6. Real footage adds motion
  blur, out-of-plane rotation (profile faces, which YuNet frequently misses),
  compression and clutter.
- **Entry exposure.** A face entering between scheduled detections is
  visible until the next one (8 frames at N = 10 on the crossing clip).
- **Track IDs are not identities.** A person who is fully hidden and
  reappears gets a new ID (1 ID switch on the crossing clip), so selective
  redaction ("leave person 2 visible") must be re-checked after occlusions
  and at scene cuts.
- **Frozen boxes.** When a tracker loses a moving face, its frozen box stays
  for 15 frames and may no longer cover the face.
- **Blur and pixelation are not anonymisation.** Only solid fill destroys
  the information, and face redaction alone does not hide clothing, voice or
  context.
- **Hardware.** Timings are from an Apple M5, faster than the target
  mid-range laptop; ratios are the transferable result.

## 9. Conclusion

Periodic detection with optical-flow tracking was faster than per-frame
detection on both clips and, where the detector missed hard poses, it was
also safer: it removed every leaked frame on the first clip. The measured
cost is exposure of faces that enter between detections, which only a
shorter interval reduces. The leak-frame metric is what made these
trade-offs visible; mean coverage stayed at or above 0.987 with LK, CSRT
and KCF on both clips.
